# Editorial Standard

## Complete editorial units

A selected clip must have:

- A natural entry point.
- Enough context to identify the subject and referents.
- A developed idea and completed factual point.
- A deliberate rhetorical or emotional landing.
- No clipped words, breaths, laughter, or unfinished gestures.
- A clear job in the larger narrative.

Do not select a passage merely because it contains the target name or term.

## Starting a clip

Avoid dependent openings such as “and,” “but,” “so,” “because,” “that,” “it,”
or “this system” unless narration or included context supplies the referent.

Prefer:

- A concise interviewer question.
- A direct thesis.
- The sentence that names the historical object.
- A clean acoustic and visual entry after irrelevant throat-clearing.

## Ending a clip

Do not stop because a keyword has been spoken, the search window ended, or a
sentence technically finished. Prefer the factual end, rhetorical landing, or
limitation that creates the next transition.

Preserve a natural tail. Never cut on the last phoneme, during a breath, or just
before a sentence that materially completes the point.

## Completeness questions

For its assigned job, the clip should answer the relevant subset of:

1. What is being discussed?
2. Who or what created it?
3. What problem did it address?
4. What was technically or historically important?
5. What limitation remained?
6. Why does it matter to the larger genealogy?

## Evidence hierarchy

Keep these categories explicit:

1. Directly supported by an original document or artifact.
2. Supported by documented correspondence.
3. First-person retrospective account.
4. Contemporaneous reporting.
5. Widely accepted interpretation.
6. Plausible inference.
7. Speculative or disputed.

Technical similarity does not prove direct influence. A later successful system
can synthesize earlier work without being reduced to copying it.

## Scoring

Score serious candidates from 1 to 10 for:

- Historical authority.
- Explanatory clarity.
- Completeness of thought.
- Natural opening.
- Strength of ending.
- Rhetorical impact.
- Audio quality.
- Visual quality.
- Uniqueness.
- Narrative usefulness.
- Accuracy.
- Brevity relative to value.

Authority can outweigh image quality when footage is historically unique.
